## Overview

Mantis Robotics has introduced the MR-X, a biomimetic dual-arm robot designed to operate without the safety fences or cages that surround most industrial machines. According to [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/mantis-robotics-launches-dual-arm-fenceless-robot/), the MR-X can lift up to 70 lb. (31.7 kg) and move at speeds of up to 10.6 m/s within a compact footprint, and the Pleasanton, California company showed it at the Automate trade show. The launch pushes the emerging category of fenceless, human-adjacent automation beyond the single-arm form factor Mantis started with.

## What We Know

The core of the machine is a system Mantis calls SafetyCore. [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/mantis-robotics-launches-dual-arm-fenceless-robot/) describes it as a "reflex system" that gives the robot "full, continuous awareness of its surroundings" and that "processes the environment in real time and reacts autonomously when a person enters the robot's path, without interrupting operations." That approach is meant to replace the external sensors and rigid keep-out zones conventional installations depend on, allowing the robot to keep running at speed even as workers move nearby.

Mantis positions the MR-X against both collaborative robots and humanoids. "Don't mistake this for another humanoid robot. It's a first-in-class dual-arm robot that outperforms both cobots and humanoids, in terms of both speed and safety," said Gerry Vannuffelen, CEO of Mantis Robotics, in the company's [announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/mantis-robotics-extends-potential-of-physical-ai-with-new-dual-arm-fenceless-robot-for-industrial-and-retail-automation-302806386.html). The company says the robot is aimed at tasks that require both strength and dexterity, including assembly, material transfer, and package sorting, per [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/mantis-robotics-launches-dual-arm-fenceless-robot/).

The MR-X builds on the company's first robot, the MR-1. That earlier single-arm machine is certified to the ISO 10218 and ISO 13849 standards and has demonstrated proven, certified fenceless operation with backing from Amazon, according to [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/mantis-robotics-launches-dual-arm-fenceless-robot/). In its announcement, Mantis said the MR-1 runs "6x faster than any cobot," framing speed as the payoff of its safety architecture, per [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/mantis-robotics-extends-potential-of-physical-ai-with-new-dual-arm-fenceless-robot-for-industrial-and-retail-automation-302806386.html).

Vannuffelen tied the new robot to a broader deployment goal. "MR-X takes that further, demonstrating that fenceless robotics is a reality regardless of form factor," he said, according to [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/mantis-robotics-launches-dual-arm-fenceless-robot/). In the company's [announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/mantis-robotics-extends-potential-of-physical-ai-with-new-dual-arm-fenceless-robot-for-industrial-and-retail-automation-302806386.html) he added: "We are not just building robots. We are building the technology that unlocks mass deployment in the real world."

## What We Don't Know

Mantis has not disclosed pricing, availability, or which customers will deploy the MR-X first. It is also unclear whether the dual-arm MR-X carries the same ISO 10218 and ISO 13849 certifications the MR-1 holds, or whether that certification work is still in progress. The company has not released independent, third-party performance or safety data for the new robot beyond its own demonstrations.

## Analysis

The pitch behind the MR-X reflects a wider shift in industrial robotics away from the trade-off that has defined the field for decades: traditional industrial arms are fast but must be caged, while collaborative robots are safe to work beside but slow. Mantis is betting that a reflex-based safety layer can collapse that distinction, letting a fast dual-arm machine share floor space with people. Whether the MR-X earns the same formal safety certifications as its predecessor, and at what cost, will determine how quickly that bet translates into deployments on real production and logistics lines.
