## Overview

Meta unveiled Muse Image, the first image-generation model built by its Meta Superintelligence Labs division, on July 7, 2026, according to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/07/meta-rolls-out-muse-a-new-ai-image-generator/) and [Meta's own announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/07/introducing-muse-image-meta-ai/). Three days later, Meta discontinued the product's most contentious capability — a feature that let any user generate AI images from another person's public Instagram photos simply by @-mentioning that account — after the move drew criticism from users and talent agencies, according to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/10/meta-removes-controversial-ai-feature-on-instagram-after-backlash/). The reversal came after CAA and SAG-AFTRA condemned the feature, according to [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/gabrielalinzainescu/2026/07/11/meta-pulled-muse-images-most-controversial-feature-the-part-that-matters-ships-anyway/).

## What We Know

Muse Image, internally code-named Mango, is now available for free through the Meta AI app, Instagram Stories and WhatsApp, according to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/07/meta-rolls-out-muse-a-new-ai-image-generator/). Meta says the model is also coming soon to Facebook and for advertisers, according to [Meta's announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/07/introducing-muse-image-meta-ai/). Use is free for "everyday creation," with a Meta subscription plan required beyond that, according to [Meta's announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/07/introducing-muse-image-meta-ai/). The model ships with more than 30 suggested presets, lets users add photos into a creation with an @-mention, supports sketch-based edits, and renders text cleanly inside generated visuals, per the same announcement.

On the technical side, Meta Superintelligence Labs describes Muse Image as an agentic system that "reflects on and improves upon its own work within its chain of thought," a self-refinement behavior the company says "emerged during RL training simply because self-refinement produced better images," according to [Meta's technical blog](https://ai.meta.com/blog/introducing-muse-image-muse-video-msl/). The model can also generate code to produce accurate plots and QR codes and can search the web to ground its outputs in factual, real-time information, per the same post. Meta says the model ranked second on the Arena leaderboard for text-to-image generation and both single- and multi-image editing as of July 5, 2026, and it built an invisible watermarking system called Content Seal, designed to persist through cropping, compression, resizing and screenshots, with a detection tool hosted at meta.ai/identification, according to [Meta's technical blog](https://ai.meta.com/blog/introducing-muse-image-muse-video-msl/).

The feature that triggered the backlash let any user reference a public Instagram account in a prompt, pulling from that account's posted photos, videos or reels to generate new images, with public accounts enabled by default, according to [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/metas-new-ai-image-tool-lets-others-use.html). Meta did not design the feature to notify an account owner when their content was used this way, according to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/07/meta-rolls-out-muse-a-new-ai-image-generator/) and [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/metas-new-ai-image-tool-lets-others-use.html). Opting out required navigating to Instagram's "Sharing and reuse" settings and toggling off both "Posts" and "Reels," and content generated before a user disabled the setting was not deleted retroactively, per [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/metas-new-ai-image-tool-lets-others-use.html). Private accounts and accounts belonging to users under 18 were automatically excluded, according to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/07/meta-rolls-out-muse-a-new-ai-image-generator/).

Meta discontinued the @-mention feature on July 10, 2026, roughly three days after launch, according to [Meta's own announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/07/introducing-muse-image-meta-ai/) and [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/gabrielalinzainescu/2026/07/11/meta-pulled-muse-images-most-controversial-feature-the-part-that-matters-ships-anyway/). The company said: "Our intent was to provide a useful creative tool and to give people control over whether their public content could be referenced in this way. We've heard the feedback that this feature missed the mark, so it's no longer available," according to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/10/meta-removes-controversial-ai-feature-on-instagram-after-backlash/), a statement corroborated by [Meta's announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/07/introducing-muse-image-meta-ai/) and [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/metas-new-ai-image-tool-lets-others-use.html).

## What We Don't Know

Neither TechCrunch nor Meta's own announcement specifies how many images were generated using other people's tagged photos during the roughly three days the feature was live, or whether Meta plans to reintroduce a version of the feature under an opt-in model. It also remains unclear whether the advertiser-facing rollout of Muse Image, which Forbes reports is still planned through Meta's Advantage+ creative tools, will include any form of the same photo-referencing mechanic.

## Analysis

The episode follows [Muse Spark](/article/2026-04/17-meta-launches-muse-spark-its-first-closed-source-ai-model-as-superintelligence-labs-bets-on-proprietary-multimodal-reasoning), Meta Superintelligence Labs' first closed-source model, which The Machine Herald previously reported on in April. Forbes frames the walk-back as narrowly targeted: the pulled feature only affected the ability to reference other users' public accounts, while the underlying Muse Image product — including its planned integration with advertiser tools through Advantage+ creative — continues unchanged, according to [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/gabrielalinzainescu/2026/07/11/meta-pulled-muse-images-most-controversial-feature-the-part-that-matters-ships-anyway/). Forbes also draws a parallel to how OpenAI responded to backlash over Sora the previous fall, per the same report. Whether Meta reintroduces a version of the photo-tagging feature in some other form remains an open question for the unit's next launch.
