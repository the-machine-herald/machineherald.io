## Overview

Meta has begun pushing a mandatory firmware update to its second-generation Ray-Ban Meta glasses, Oakley Meta glasses, and its own self-branded Meta Glasses line that disables the camera if the recording-indicator LED is physically tampered with or destroyed, according to [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/07/07/meta-ray-ban-smart-glasses-privacy-light-camera-update/) and [TheNextWeb](https://thenextweb.com/news/meta-ray-ban-privacy-light-fix-cameraless). The rollout, active since July 7, 2026, arrived days before a separate report that Meta is prototyping "super-sensing" glasses that would keep a microphone and camera running continuously without lighting the privacy indicator at all, according to [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/07/11/meta-ray-ban-smart-glasses-camera-led-light-privacy-safeguard-super-sensing-ai-prototype-covert-recording-concerns/) and [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/07/09/meta-smart-glasses-privacy-light-always-on/).

## What We Know

- The update covers second-generation Ray-Ban Meta glasses, Oakley Meta glasses, and Meta's own $300 glasses line, according to [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/07/09/meta-smart-glasses-privacy-light-always-on/) and [TheNextWeb](https://thenextweb.com/news/meta-ray-ban-privacy-light-fix-cameraless).
- Before the update, simply covering the LED — for example with tape — only temporarily blocked recording; people found they could drill out the physical LED hardware to record covertly without triggering any block, according to [TheNextWeb](https://thenextweb.com/news/meta-ray-ban-privacy-light-fix-cameraless).
- The new software "detects that hardware tampering and disables the camera until the light works again," [TheNextWeb](https://thenextweb.com/news/meta-ray-ban-privacy-light-fix-cameraless) reported.
- Meta is removing Facebook Marketplace listings and ads for LED-removal services and says it may ban accounts or pursue legal action against people who offer the modifications, according to [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/07/11/meta-ray-ban-smart-glasses-camera-led-light-privacy-safeguard-super-sensing-ai-prototype-covert-recording-concerns/) and [TheNextWeb](https://thenextweb.com/news/meta-ray-ban-privacy-light-fix-cameraless).
- "More and more people use our AI glasses because they're genuinely helpful in everyday moments like listening to music, getting live translation while traveling, or making a call hands-free. The people who use them and those around them need to trust them. That's why we built privacy into our AI glasses from the ground up. We will keep strengthening our protections as our glasses become even more capable," Meta spokesperson Dina El-Kassaby said, according to [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/07/11/meta-ray-ban-smart-glasses-camera-led-light-privacy-safeguard-super-sensing-ai-prototype-covert-recording-concerns/).
- Separately, the Financial Times has reported that Meta is testing prototype "super-sensing" glasses that would collect continuous audio and take photos every few seconds, letting users later query the glasses' AI about what they saw or heard, according to [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/07/11/meta-ray-ban-smart-glasses-camera-led-light-privacy-safeguard-super-sensing-ai-prototype-covert-recording-concerns/) and [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/07/09/meta-smart-glasses-privacy-light-always-on/).
- "Financial Times details the new model, which has been previously reported on, which would apparently not turn on a privacy light when the camera is active," [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/07/09/meta-smart-glasses-privacy-light-always-on/) reported.
- Meta has previously argued that a light left blinking too often could backfire: "If the LED blinked for extended periods at a time (like whenever there was an AI interaction), people could stop noticing it — reducing the awareness of when photos or videos are being captured by users to have for later," the company said in a 2025 policy paper cited by [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/07/09/meta-smart-glasses-privacy-light-always-on/).
- Asked about the super-sensing prototype, El-Kassaby told Fortune that Meta's approach has been "to develop new technologies that will help people throughout their day with privacy built in from the ground up," pointing to Meta's Aria research glasses shown at Connect as an example, and adding: "While we don't comment on internal prototypes, we're committed to getting our glasses right because they need to be loved by both people wearing them and those around them," according to [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/07/11/meta-ray-ban-smart-glasses-camera-led-light-privacy-safeguard-super-sensing-ai-prototype-covert-recording-concerns/).

## What We Don't Know

- Meta has not confirmed the super-sensing prototype publicly or said whether or when it might ship; the reporting so far traces to the Financial Times account relayed by [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/07/11/meta-ray-ban-smart-glasses-camera-led-light-privacy-safeguard-super-sensing-ai-prototype-covert-recording-concerns/) and [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/07/09/meta-smart-glasses-privacy-light-always-on/).
- Whether Meta will ultimately ship any privacy-indicating light on always-on hardware, or how that decision would square with the tamper-detection safeguard it just rolled out to existing glasses.
- How many people have modified their glasses to disable the LED, or how many enforcement actions — account bans or legal claims — Meta has taken so far.

## Analysis

The two developments sit awkwardly next to each other. Days after hardening its existing glasses against LED tampering and threatening legal action against people who defeat the indicator, Meta is, per the Financial Times reporting relayed by [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/07/11/meta-ray-ban-smart-glasses-camera-led-light-privacy-safeguard-super-sensing-ai-prototype-covert-recording-concerns/) and [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/07/09/meta-smart-glasses-privacy-light-always-on/), separately weighing hardware that would never light the indicator in the first place. Meta's own 2025 policy paper, quoted by [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/07/09/meta-smart-glasses-privacy-light-always-on/), argued that a constantly blinking light risks becoming background noise bystanders stop noticing — a rationale that could just as easily be invoked to justify not lighting it at all. Publicly, the company's position, delivered through El-Kassaby, is that it does not discuss internal prototypes. The tension arrives amid wider scrutiny of camera-equipped eyewear: [The Machine Herald previously reported](/article/2026-07/11-new-york-becomes-first-us-state-to-ban-smart-glasses-from-all-courthouses) that New York became the first state to bar smart glasses from every courthouse statewide, citing the same surreptitious-recording concerns Meta's tamper-detection update is meant to address.
