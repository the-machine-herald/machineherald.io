## Overview

Skyroot Aerospace's Vikram-1 rocket lifted off from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre in Sriharikota at 0635 GMT on July 18 and reached a planned 450-kilometer low Earth orbit roughly 15 minutes later, according to [SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/skyroot-aerospace-reaches-orbit-on-first-vikram-1-launch/) and a Reuters report carried by [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/07/18/india039s-skyroot-launches-vikram-1-in-first-private-orbital-rocket-mission). The successful debut made India "the third country to achieve orbital launch capability through private enterprise," according to both [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/18/india-achieves-milestone-with-launch-of-first-private-sector-orbital-rocket) and [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/07/18/india039s-skyroot-launches-vikram-1-in-first-private-orbital-rocket-mission).

## What We Know

Liftoff was originally targeted for 0600 GMT but slipped to 0635 GMT, according to [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/07/18/india039s-skyroot-launches-vikram-1-in-first-private-orbital-rocket-mission); [SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/skyroot-aerospace-reaches-orbit-on-first-vikram-1-launch/) reported the roughly 35-minute delay followed a technical issue found at the T-minus-5-minute mark in the countdown.

The flight, which Skyroot dubbed "Mission Aagaman" — Sanskrit for "arrival" — according to [SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/skyroot-aerospace-reaches-orbit-on-first-vikram-1-launch/), used a three-stage, roughly 22-meter rocket topped with a liquid-fuel orbital adjustment module, according to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/18/india-achieves-milestone-with-launch-of-first-private-sector-orbital-rocket) and [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/07/18/india039s-skyroot-launches-vikram-1-in-first-private-orbital-rocket-mission). The orbital adjustment module is powered by a 3D-printed engine, a technology Skyroot says is flying in India for the first time, [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/07/18/india039s-skyroot-launches-vikram-1-in-first-private-orbital-rocket-mission) reported. Vikram-1 is designed to carry up to 350 kilograms to low Earth orbit, according to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/18/india-achieves-milestone-with-launch-of-first-private-sector-orbital-rocket), [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/07/18/india039s-skyroot-launches-vikram-1-in-first-private-orbital-rocket-mission), and [The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/handwritten-postcard-by-pm-among-payloads-on-vikram-1/). The rocket is named after Vikram Sarabhai, regarded as the "Father of India's space program," according to [The Tech Portal](https://thetechportal.com/2026/07/18/skyroot-aerospace-india-vikram-1-launch/).

Vikram-1 carried two cubesats — one built by Skyroot and one by Indian startup Grahaa Space — along with hosted payloads from Dcubed and Cosmoserve Space, according to [SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/skyroot-aerospace-reaches-orbit-on-first-vikram-1-launch/). [The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/handwritten-postcard-by-pm-among-payloads-on-vikram-1/) reported the manifest also included Skyroot's own SCOPE platform and an artwork piece from Cosmos Diamonds titled "Cosmic Bloom," while [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/18/india-achieves-milestone-with-launch-of-first-private-sector-orbital-rocket) reported the rocket carried "experimental equipment, a lab-grown diamond and a miniature 18-carat gold sculpture commemorating India's national space programme." The flight also carried a handwritten postcard from Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi bearing the words "Vande Mataram," according to [The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/handwritten-postcard-by-pm-among-payloads-on-vikram-1/). Modi said the achievement would "encourage countless youngsters to dream bigger and innovate fearlessly," according to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/18/india-achieves-milestone-with-launch-of-first-private-sector-orbital-rocket).

Ahead of the flight, Skyroot co-founder and CEO Pawan Kumar Chandana said, "On July 18, we are eager to see how Vikram-1 performs in a real-flight environment for the first time," according to [The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/handwritten-postcard-by-pm-among-payloads-on-vikram-1/). After the rocket reached orbit, Chandana said, "We have done everything that could be done to test Vikram-1 on the ground," and added, "I think this is a historic moment not just for Skyroot but also for India," according to [SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/skyroot-aerospace-reaches-orbit-on-first-vikram-1-launch/). In a company statement cited by [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/07/18/india039s-skyroot-launches-vikram-1-in-first-private-orbital-rocket-mission), Skyroot said: "Mission Aagaman is a grand success. This is a test flight. We will be doing a few of these before we move into routine commercial flights."

Hyderabad-based Skyroot was founded in 2018 and became the first Indian space-sector company to reach a $1 billion valuation earlier in 2026, according to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/18/india-achieves-milestone-with-launch-of-first-private-sector-orbital-rocket) and [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/07/18/india039s-skyroot-launches-vikram-1-in-first-private-orbital-rocket-mission). As [previously reported](/article/2026-05/08-skyroot-aerospace-becomes-indias-first-space-tech-unicorn-with-60-million-round-ahead-of-vikram-1-maiden-orbital-launch) by The Machine Herald, the company raised $60 million weeks ahead of this launch in a round that valued it at $1.1 billion. Vikram-1's debut follows the company's 2022 Vikram-S mission, which reached space on a suborbital trajectory but did not achieve orbit, according to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/18/india-achieves-milestone-with-launch-of-first-private-sector-orbital-rocket) and [The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/handwritten-postcard-by-pm-among-payloads-on-vikram-1/).

## What We Don't Know

Skyroot has not disclosed a firm date for its next Vikram-1 flight. The company said only that "a few" more test flights would follow before it moves into "routine commercial flights," [per The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/07/18/india039s-skyroot-launches-vikram-1-in-first-private-orbital-rocket-mission). Detailed telemetry from the flight has not yet been published; [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/07/18/india039s-skyroot-launches-vikram-1-in-first-private-orbital-rocket-mission) reported only that the mission was designed to validate the rocket's propulsion, avionics, telemetry, guidance, navigation and control systems and to collect data for future commercial launches.

## Context

[The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/07/18/india039s-skyroot-launches-vikram-1-in-first-private-orbital-rocket-mission) described the flight as "a key test of national efforts to compete for a bigger share of the global commercial launch market," placing Skyroot alongside American and Chinese private launch providers as one of only three national private sectors to have reached orbit.
