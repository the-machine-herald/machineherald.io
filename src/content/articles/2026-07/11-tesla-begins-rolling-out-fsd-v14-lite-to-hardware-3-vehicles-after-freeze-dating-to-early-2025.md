## Overview

Tesla has begun rolling out FSD v14 Lite to Hardware 3 (HW3) vehicles, delivering the first major Full Self-Driving update to roughly 4 million older cars that had been frozen on FSD v12.6 since early 2025, according to [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/06/29/tesla-fsd-v14-lite-hw3-rollout/). The rollout, which started June 29, 2026 with firmware version 2026.20.5.1, follows [The Machine Herald's earlier report](/article/2026-04/25-tesla-admits-hardware-3-cannot-support-unsupervised-full-self-driving-plans-micro-factories-to-retrofit-millions-of-vehicles) that Tesla CEO Elon Musk admitted on the company's Q1 2026 earnings call that HW3 hardware cannot support unsupervised FSD, and that a scaled-down v14 update was expected for the older hardware.

## What We Know

**The rollout.** The update is going out first to Tesla's early-access drivers, according to [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/06/29/tesla-fsd-v14-lite-hw3-rollout/). Hardware 3, introduced in 2019, powers vehicles from roughly 2019 to early 2023 models, according to [Teslarati](https://www.teslarati.com/tesla-gives-hw3-owners-another-massive-update/).

**What Tesla says about it.** Ashok Elluswamy, Tesla's Head of AI, said the update "includes destination options and speed profiles on city roads, but more importantly significantly improved safety," adding: "We hope you'll enjoy it, once the build ships wide," according to [Teslarati](https://www.teslarati.com/tesla-full-self-driving-v14-lite-release-notes/).

**New capabilities.** Teslarati reported the update introduces parking, unparking, and reversing capabilities, along with Arrival Options for selecting a drop-off location (Parking Lot, Street, Driveway, or Curbside) and Speed Profiles available at all times, according to [Teslarati](https://www.teslarati.com/tesla-full-self-driving-v14-lite-release-notes/). The outlet also reported improved responsiveness in navigation, merges, forks, pedestrian interactions, and traffic lights, along with fewer false slowdowns and smoother steering, according to [Teslarati](https://www.teslarati.com/tesla-full-self-driving-v14-lite-release-notes/).

**Still Level 2.** Despite the upgrade, the system remains "a Level 2 driver-assistance system that requires the driver to pay attention and keep their hands ready at all times," according to [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/06/29/tesla-fsd-v14-lite-hw3-rollout/). It does not deliver the unsupervised autonomy that HW3 owners were originally sold. The hardware gap driving that limitation is significant: "the chip has roughly one-eighth the memory bandwidth of HW4," according to [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/06/29/tesla-fsd-v14-lite-hw3-rollout/).

**Legal pressure builds in the Netherlands.** A Dutch collective claim against Tesla over the broken "Full Self-Driving" promise on Hardware 3 vehicles "has reached nearly 7,000 verified participants," according to [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/06/18/tesla-hw3-claim-netherlands-7000-owners-kennedy-van-der-laan/). The initiative, launched by Dutch Tesla owner Mischa Sigtermans, started in April with 3,000 signups in its first week, according to [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/06/18/tesla-hw3-claim-netherlands-7000-owners-kennedy-van-der-laan/). Case preparation is now backed by Kennedy Van der Laan, described by Electrek as "one of the Netherlands' most respected law firms," according to [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/06/18/tesla-hw3-claim-netherlands-7000-owners-kennedy-van-der-laan/). Owners who separately paid for the Full Self-Driving package want a refund of that cost, which "cost up to €6,400 in the Netherlands," according to [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/06/18/tesla-hw3-claim-netherlands-7000-owners-kennedy-van-der-laan/).

**International expansion plans.** Tesla has stated that "following future rollout of FSD V14 Lite for HW3 vehicles in the US, we plan on expanding V14 Lite to additional international markets," according to [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/04/28/tesla-promises-fsd-v14-lite-hw3-international-markets/). The company cautioned that "international rollout is subject to several factors (completion of technical verification, regional adaptation & relevant regulatory approvals), we can't provide definitive dates at the moment," according to [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/04/28/tesla-promises-fsd-v14-lite-hw3-international-markets/), a hedge Teslarati also reported in late April, according to [Teslarati](https://www.teslarati.com/tesla-gives-hw3-owners-another-massive-update/).

## What We Don't Know

- **International timeline.** Tesla has not given a definitive date for when FSD v14 Lite will reach HW3 vehicles outside the United States.
- **Outcome of the Netherlands claim.** No ruling or formal settlement has been reported for the collective action covering the nearly 7,000 participants.
- **Hardware retrofit timeline.** Tesla's previously announced plan to physically upgrade HW3 vehicles through dedicated micro-factories has not been updated with new dates since the April announcement.
- **Broader release timing in the US.** Beyond the initial early-access rollout, Tesla has not specified when the wider US HW3 fleet will receive the update.
