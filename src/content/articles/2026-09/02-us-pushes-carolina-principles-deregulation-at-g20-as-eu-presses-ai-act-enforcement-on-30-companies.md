## Overview

The United States and the European Union staked out opposing positions on artificial intelligence regulation this week, as G20 economic ministers met in North Carolina, according to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/2/us-pushes-looser-approach-to-ai-regulation-while-eu-pushes-new-law) and [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/trump-admin-to-tell-g20-governments-to-back-off-from-regulating-ai-report-says-2000805455). Washington promoted a light-touch framework it calls the "Carolina Principles," while Brussels pressed ahead with compliance checks on dozens of AI companies under its AI Act.

## What We Know

- At a Group of 20 ministerial meeting hosted by the US in Chapel Hill, North Carolina, on Tuesday, Washington argued against AI-specific regulations, according to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/2/us-pushes-looser-approach-to-ai-regulation-while-eu-pushes-new-law).
- The gathering ran on two tracks: finance ministers met in Asheville, while trade and commerce ministers convened in Raleigh-Durham for an "Innovation Ministerial" co-hosted by Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and presidential tech adviser Michael Kratsios, according to [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/trump-admin-to-tell-g20-governments-to-back-off-from-regulating-ai-report-says-2000805455).
- Kratsios promoted the "Carolina Principles," which call for regulations that don't target particular technologies. "Policymakers do not need to approach each innovation in isolation and should not treat every emerging technology as a first-of-a-kind policy problem," he said, according to [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/trump-admin-to-tell-g20-governments-to-back-off-from-regulating-ai-report-says-2000805455).
- Under the framework, signatory countries would commit to investing in "foundational research to accelerate discovery," strengthening commercial AI opportunities, and would "reserve new regulation for novel considerations," according to [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/trump-admin-to-tell-g20-governments-to-back-off-from-regulating-ai-report-says-2000805455).
- Top tech leaders attended the conference, including Meta's Mark Zuckerberg and Tesla's Elon Musk, according to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/2/us-pushes-looser-approach-to-ai-regulation-while-eu-pushes-new-law). Lutnick was also set to host discussions with OpenAI CEO Sam Altman and Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, while Kratsios indicated that Musk, Google DeepMind's Demis Hassabis, venture capitalist David Sacks and Zuckerberg would address attendees, according to [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/trump-admin-to-tell-g20-governments-to-back-off-from-regulating-ai-report-says-2000805455).
- Zuckerberg highlighted the challenge of hiring workers for data center expansion, saying his company needed "hundreds of thousands, and maybe millions" of skilled tradespeople, according to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/2/us-pushes-looser-approach-to-ai-regulation-while-eu-pushes-new-law).
- Musk stressed energy demands, warning "there will be a significant power shortfall next year," and criticized European regulation, arguing innovation requires entrepreneurs be "relatively free of regulation, meaning that new things must be default legal," according to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/2/us-pushes-looser-approach-to-ai-regulation-while-eu-pushes-new-law).
- Canada's delegation planned to advocate for balancing innovation with "public trust and safety," a contrast with the US position, according to [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/trump-admin-to-tell-g20-governments-to-back-off-from-regulating-ai-report-says-2000805455).
- Separately, the European Commission sent information requests to more than 30 AI companies worldwide, focusing on safety and copyright compliance under the EU's AI Act, according to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/2/us-pushes-looser-approach-to-ai-regulation-while-eu-pushes-new-law).

## What We Don't Know

- Which other G20 members, if any, intend to sign onto the Carolina Principles has not been reported.
- Whether the European Commission's information requests to the more than 30 AI companies will lead to fines or corrective orders under the AI Act is not yet known.
- How Canada's push for a "public trust and safety" balance will factor into any formal outcome from the meeting remains unclear.
