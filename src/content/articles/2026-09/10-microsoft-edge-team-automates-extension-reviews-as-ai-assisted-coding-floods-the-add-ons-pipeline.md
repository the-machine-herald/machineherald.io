## Overview

Microsoft says the rise of AI-assisted coding is flooding its Edge Add-ons store with new extension submissions, and it is responding by automating parts of its review pipeline, according to a [Microsoft Edge Team blog post](https://blogs.windows.com/msedgedev/2026/09/08/faster-reviews-and-quality-recognition-for-microsoft-edge-extensions/). "Rapid adoption of AI-assisted coding is enabling developers to build extensions faster than ever," the team wrote, adding that "More developers are building, iterating, and submitting extensions, which is great for the ecosystem and ultimately gives users more choice," according to [Microsoft](https://blogs.windows.com/msedgedev/2026/09/08/faster-reviews-and-quality-recognition-for-microsoft-edge-extensions/).

## What We Know

Microsoft frames that growth as a tension between keeping pace with the ecosystem's expansion and maintaining a high bar for quality, according to [Microsoft](https://blogs.windows.com/msedgedev/2026/09/08/faster-reviews-and-quality-recognition-for-microsoft-edge-extensions/). The company had already introduced an expedited review track for high-quality and high-value extensions, but that process is now under strain too: "Since then, submission volumes have continued to increase, and our review pipeline has experienced additional strain, causing an increase in extension review turnaround time," the Edge team said, as quoted by both [Microsoft](https://blogs.windows.com/msedgedev/2026/09/08/faster-reviews-and-quality-recognition-for-microsoft-edge-extensions/) and [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/09/09/another-microsoft-team-admits-its-struggling-to-handle-flood-of-ai-generated-code/5295185).

To cut turnaround time, Microsoft says it has "introduced automation for many of the repeatable validation checks that are part of the review process and streamlined how reviews move through our pipeline," according to [Microsoft](https://blogs.windows.com/msedgedev/2026/09/08/faster-reviews-and-quality-recognition-for-microsoft-edge-extensions/). The company frames the change as additive rather than a lowering of the bar: "This doesn't change our review standards or reduce the checks an extension must pass," the Edge team said. "Instead, it helps us identify known policy violations and security issues more consistently while allowing reviewers to spend more time on complex cases that benefit from human judgment," according to [Microsoft](https://blogs.windows.com/msedgedev/2026/09/08/faster-reviews-and-quality-recognition-for-microsoft-edge-extensions/) and [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/09/09/another-microsoft-team-admits-its-struggling-to-handle-flood-of-ai-generated-code/5295185). Notably, Microsoft's post does not say whether the new automation itself relies on AI, a gap [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/09/09/another-microsoft-team-admits-its-struggling-to-handle-flood-of-ai-generated-code/5295185) flagged directly, noting the Edge team explained its automation "without revealing if that automation uses AI."

Microsoft is also changing how it recognizes quality. The "Featured" badge on the Edge Add-ons site is meant to flag extensions with "a strong commitment to quality, reliability, security, and user experience," and eligibility is judged against "more than 60 quality signals across multiple dimensions of an extension," with most of that evaluation automated "to make it more consistent, objective, and scalable," according to [Microsoft](https://blogs.windows.com/msedgedev/2026/09/08/faster-reviews-and-quality-recognition-for-microsoft-edge-extensions/). Microsoft says it will now refresh Featured badges every 15 days, up from a slower cadence, so that "high-quality extensions can earn recognition sooner, and developers receive faster feedback on their investments in quality," according to both [Microsoft](https://blogs.windows.com/msedgedev/2026/09/08/faster-reviews-and-quality-recognition-for-microsoft-edge-extensions/) and [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/09/09/another-microsoft-team-admits-its-struggling-to-handle-flood-of-ai-generated-code/5295185).

[The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/09/09/another-microsoft-team-admits-its-struggling-to-handle-flood-of-ai-generated-code/5295185) situates the Edge team's post as the second time in recent weeks that a Microsoft team has publicly described being overwhelmed by AI-related workload, noting it had separately "spotted Microsoft's Exchange team admitting that AI has found so many bugs it hasn't found time to deliver a Cumulative Update for the messaging server." The outlet also argued that Microsoft's review backlog reflects "more work to do – but fewer people to do it with thanks to regular rounds of redundancies" — its own characterization rather than a claim Microsoft made in its post.

## What We Don't Know

Microsoft has not said whether its new review automation itself uses AI models, nor has it disclosed current submission volumes, backlog size, or how much turnaround times had grown before the change. The company also has not detailed what the Featured badge refresh cadence was prior to the move to 15 days.

## Analysis

The episode is a small but concrete data point in a broader pattern The Machine Herald has tracked through 2026: AI-assisted coding tools are increasing the volume of code and submissions arriving at review chokepoints faster than the humans and processes built to vet that code can keep up, pushing platform operators toward more automation in the review step itself — even as, in Microsoft's case, the company declines to say whether that new automation is itself AI-driven.
