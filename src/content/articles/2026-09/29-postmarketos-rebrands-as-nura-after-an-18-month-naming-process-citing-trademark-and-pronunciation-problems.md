## Overview

The mobile Linux operating system postmarketOS has officially changed its name to Nura, according to [Linuxiac](https://linuxiac.com/postmarketos-is-now-nura-after-major-project-rebrand/), ending a naming process that lasted roughly a year and a half. Linuxiac reports that the project announced the new name during its conference, and that references to postmarketOS will remain in documentation, packages, websites, and other places while the transition completes.

## What We Know

- **The name.** Nura is short for "Nuraghe," the name given to ancient stone structures found across Sardinia, Italy, [Linuxiac](https://linuxiac.com/postmarketos-is-now-nura-after-major-project-rebrand/) reports. The developers say this fits the project's long-standing goal of keeping devices useful for as long as possible.
- **Why rename.** According to [Linuxiac](https://linuxiac.com/postmarketos-is-now-nura-after-major-project-rebrand/), the team points to the old name being long and hard to pronounce and remember in some languages, and to its unusual capitalization causing confusion, with variants such as PostmarketOS and PostMarketOS commonly appearing.
- **Scope of the old name.** Linuxiac reports that the descriptive old name no longer reflected the project's scope, and that PinePhones are now sold with postmarketOS preinstalled, which made a strictly "post-market" framing less applicable.
- **Trademark.** Because postmarketOS is a descriptive name, it cannot be trademarked, [Linuxiac](https://linuxiac.com/postmarketos-is-now-nura-after-major-project-rebrand/) reports. A trademark application for Nura has been filed, which the outlet says gives the project greater ability to protect its identity against fraudulent or misleading use.
- **Selection process.** More than 300 names were submitted by the community before a selection team narrowed them down through multiple evaluation rounds, according to [Linuxiac](https://linuxiac.com/postmarketos-is-now-nura-after-major-project-rebrand/). Four candidates reached the final shortlist, were checked for potentially problematic meanings across different languages, and went to an internal team vote, where Nura received the highest result.
- **Timeline.** [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2026/9/postmarketos-rebrands-as-nura-in-move-to-broaden-appeal-beyond-tech-community-keeps-logo/) reports that the rebranding was first teased in March 2025, and that delays tied to trademark considerations and the need to build consensus within the community pushed the official reveal back.
- **Logo and domain.** The familiar logo stays, with refinements including increased spacing between its arrows and a mathematical reconstruction to make it easier to reproduce consistently, per [Linuxiac](https://linuxiac.com/postmarketos-is-now-nura-after-major-project-rebrand/). The project's new home is nura.eco; Linuxiac says the developers first looked at nura.org, but the domain was already owned and could not be purchased at a reasonable price. [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2026/9/postmarketos-rebrands-as-nura-in-move-to-broaden-appeal-beyond-tech-community-keeps-logo/) adds that the .eco extension aligns with the project's environmental mission.

## Stated Goal

AlternativeTo reports that the team aims to expand recognition beyond the traditional hacker community that has supported the project since its inception.

## What Changes for Users

[Linuxiac](https://linuxiac.com/postmarketos-is-now-nura-after-major-project-rebrand/) states that for end users, nothing changes: Nura continues the same mission postmarketOS has pursued, providing a Linux-based operating system that can keep smartphones and other devices useful long after their original software support has ended.

## What We Don't Know

The sources reviewed do not say when the remaining postmarketOS references in documentation, packages, and websites will be fully replaced, nor whether package or device names will change. The trademark application for Nura has been filed, but no outcome has been reported. Coverage so far relies on the project's own account of its reasoning, and no independent assessment of whether the rebrand will widen the project's audience is available yet.

## Analysis

The reasoning laid out in the coverage is practical rather than cosmetic: a name that can be trademarked, spelled consistently, and pronounced across languages. The stated intent to reach beyond the hacker community fits with the pre-installed-hardware sales that the PinePhone example illustrates, though the coverage reviewed does not describe any further hardware partnerships.
