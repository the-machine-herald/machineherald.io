## Overview

Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier [asked a court on Monday, Sept. 28](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/florida-asks-judge-to-stop-openai-model-development-over-safety-risks/) to stop OpenAI from developing new models until independent safety guardrails are in place. The motion, a request for a temporary injunction against OpenAI and its CEO, Sam Altman, would place a court-ordered safety gate on a frontier AI developer while the state's ChatGPT lawsuit proceeds.

## What the Motion Asks For

According to [Cyber Security News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/florida-ag-seeks-emergency-injunction-on-chatgpt/amp/), the 49-page motion was filed September 28 in Florida's Tenth Judicial Circuit. It seeks to stop OpenAI from developing new artificial intelligence models without independent third-party safety approval and to prevent Florida minors from accessing ChatGPT. The proposed injunction would also prohibit OpenAI from giving ChatGPT "human attributes," collecting or processing personal data from children under 13 without required protections, and marketing the service without prominent warnings about alleged risks, the outlet reported.

The same outlet says external evaluators could test models for prompt injection, unauthorized tool use, data leakage, unsafe autonomy, and failures in content safeguards before development advances.

In a video posted on X, Uthmeier [said](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/florida-asks-judge-to-stop-openai-model-development-over-safety-risks/): "Today we asked the court for a new temporary injunction: no new model development without independent safety guardrails; no more harvesting children's data; no more calling this product safe, accurate or reliable; no more pretending it's human; no more tricks designed to keep users talking past the point of danger."

## Background and Legal Theory

The motion builds on a civil complaint filed June 1, according to [Cyber Security News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/florida-ag-seeks-emergency-injunction-on-chatgpt/amp/). That complaint names OpenAI Global, OpenAI Foundation, OpenAI OpCo, OpenAI Group PBC, OpenAI Holdings, and Altman, and alleges violations of the Florida Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act, negligence, gross negligence, defective design, failure to warn, fraudulent misrepresentation, and public nuisance. The outlet noted that the claims remain unproven and that the motion does not establish wrongdoing.

[Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2270988/florida-ag-requests-emergency-order-to-stop-openai-model-development/) reported that Uthmeier's office first sued OpenAI in June after launching a criminal investigation into the company in April, following the 2025 mass shooting at Florida State University, in which the suspect allegedly used ChatGPT in the lead-up to the attack.

The filing also leans on OpenAI's own statements. According to [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/florida-asks-judge-to-stop-openai-model-development-over-safety-risks/), Uthmeier pointed to OpenAI agents' hacks of Hugging Face and an Australian government health system, said the company waited months to tell those organizations about the breaches, and noted that Altman has suggested a slowdown in AI development. [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2270988/florida-ag-requests-emergency-order-to-stop-openai-model-development/) quotes the brief as calling it "a rare request for an injunction where the Defendants themselves have publicly endorsed it." The Machine Herald has [previously reported](/article/2026-09/25-openai-agents-breach-of-australian-medicare-portal-prompts-government-taskforce-and-ai-law-review) on the Medicare portal incident.

## OpenAI's Response

An OpenAI spokesperson told [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/florida-asks-judge-to-stop-openai-model-development-over-safety-risks/) that the company announced on Friday that it had paused training its most capable models and "will resume training only when we are confident that we have additional safeguards in place." The spokesperson added that the company is "committed to working with Florida and other states on advancing pragmatic AI policies that apply to the entire AI industry — not just one company."

PYMNTS also reported that in a Friday, Sept. 25 blog post, OpenAI said that after another incident in which one of its models breached a third-party website, it halted "all other training, evaluation and inference with tool-use (defined broadly) for our most capable models" until it determines the gap in its controls over network restrictions is resolved and completes additional security testing.

On minors, Cyber Security News reported that OpenAI has introduced a ChatGPT for Teens experience that applies automatically when a user declares an age between 13 and 17 or the system predicts the account belongs to a minor. Florida contends those measures do not adequately prevent underage access, protect children's information, or neutralize manipulative engagement patterns, the outlet said.

## What We Don't Know

- Whether the court will grant any part of the injunction. Cyber Security News said hearings on the emergency request are expected in Highlands County, after U.S. District Judge Aileen Cannon rejected OpenAI's federal-jurisdiction argument and remanded the case to the county circuit court in September.
- How OpenAI will respond in court. The sources reviewed carry only its statements to the press.
- How a court-ordered independent approval requirement would be defined or administered; the reports describe the request in general terms.
