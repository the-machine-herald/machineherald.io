## Overview

OpenAI has announced that it will add invisible watermarks to text generated by ChatGPT and Codex in the European Union, according to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/openai-will-start-watermarking-chatgpts-text-in-the-eu/). The company is doing so to comply with the EU AI Act's transparency rules. For software teams, the notable detail is that the feature is also available to API developers outside Europe as an opt-in.

## What We Know

- **Scope and timing.** The watermark will roll out over the coming weeks to eligible ChatGPT and Codex users on all plans in the EU, [TechCrunch reported](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/openai-will-start-watermarking-chatgpts-text-in-the-eu/).
- **API opt-in.** According to TechCrunch, developers using OpenAI's API anywhere in the world can turn the feature on for select models starting immediately, and it is off by default.
- **How it works.** The method is called textGrain. It shapes the model's word choices so that the text carries a pattern readers cannot see but a detector can pick up, per [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/openai-will-start-watermarking-chatgpts-text-in-the-eu/). OpenAI says the watermark does not identify users and has no meaningful effect on model performance, the same report states.
- **Limits.** Testing described by TechCrunch found that replacing 10% of words with synonyms lowered detection from about 92% to 66%. Short passages, math answers and translated text are harder to detect. OpenAI also says that a missing watermark does not prove a text was written by a human.
- **Detector access.** Access to the detector will initially be limited to approved researchers, according to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/openai-will-start-watermarking-chatgpts-text-in-the-eu/).

## The Regulatory Background

The EU AI Act's transparency rules took effect on August 2 and require AI companies to mark AI-generated content in a way other systems can identify, [TechCrunch noted](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/openai-will-start-watermarking-chatgpts-text-in-the-eu/). The European Commission's guidance states that Article 50 of the AI Act applies from 2 August 2026 and that providers must add machine-readable marks to enable the detection of AI-generated or manipulated content, according to the [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-ai-transparency-obligations). Providers that choose approaches other than the voluntary Code of Practice on transparency must show equivalently adequate means of meeting the marking obligations, the Commission page adds. The rules were covered in The Machine Herald's [earlier report on Article 50 taking effect](/article/2026-08/02-eu-ai-act-transparency-rules-take-effect-requiring-deepfake-and-ai-content-labeling-across-the-bloc).

## How This Compares With Anthropic

Anthropic announced its own text watermarking in August. [TechCrunch reported](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/11/anthropic-says-it-will-watermark-text-generated-by-its-ai-models/) that the company would apply it starting with models released after August 2, use the C2PA open standard for files, and extend the approach to older models, across its API, Claude Code, Claude Cowork and Claude Tag. TechCrunch's October report says Anthropic is applying its watermarking worldwide, whereas OpenAI's default rollout is limited to the EU, with the API option available elsewhere. In the August article, TechCrunch relayed Anthropic's statement that the watermark travels with copied text and may persist through some editing, and noted that the amount of editing needed to remove it remained unclear.

## What We Don't Know

- OpenAI's own announcement page could not be retrieved for this report, so the list of API models that support the option is not confirmed here.
- It is unclear how many third-party tools that rewrite or translate model output will preserve a watermark, given the detection drop reported for synonym substitution and translated text.
- The reports reviewed do not say whether regulators have assessed textGrain against the Article 50 marking requirement.
- Whether OpenAI will extend the default-on behavior beyond the EU has not been stated in the sources reviewed.

## Analysis

The reported detection figures illustrate a practical constraint for developers: statistical text watermarks are probabilistic signals rather than proof of provenance. Teams building pipelines that edit, summarize or translate model output may see markers weaken, and a missing mark, by OpenAI's own account, says little about authorship. The disclosure of those limits alongside the rollout matters for anyone deciding whether to enable the API option.
