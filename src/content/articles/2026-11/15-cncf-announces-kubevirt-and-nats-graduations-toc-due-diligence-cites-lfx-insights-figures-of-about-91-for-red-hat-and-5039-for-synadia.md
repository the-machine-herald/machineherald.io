## Overview

The Cloud Native Computing Foundation (CNCF) announced the graduation of [KubeVirt](https://www.cncf.io/announcements/2026/11/10/cloud-native-computing-foundation-announces-kubevirt-graduation/), which adds virtual machine management to Kubernetes, and of [NATS](https://www.cncf.io/announcements/2026/11/10/cncf-announces-nats-graduation-solidifying-a-production-ready-foundation-for-messaging-across-cloud-and-edge/), a messaging and streaming system, in two releases dated November 10, 2026 and issued at KubeCon + CloudNativeCon North America in Salt Lake City. The Technical Oversight Committee (TOC) votes had already closed on November 2 (NATS) and November 4 (KubeVirt), according to the [NATS](https://github.com/cncf/toc/issues/2042) and [KubeVirt](https://github.com/cncf/toc/issues/1822) application issues. Two due diligence documents in the TOC's public repository carry contribution-concentration figures that neither announcement gives: the [KubeVirt document](https://github.com/cncf/toc/blob/66896801e461d70b6492c1830a17d57f5b7b52ed/projects/kubevirt/kubevirt-graduation-due-diligence.md) says six of eight maintainers work for Red Hat, and the [NATS document](https://github.com/cncf/toc/blob/4c83a0f63116b98fefc0c63862bbd1b51558c53c/projects/nats/nats-graduation-dd.md) cites an LFX Insights organization leaderboard that lists Synadia at 50.39%.

## What CNCF announced

The KubeVirt release says Red Hat created the project in 2016, that it entered the CNCF Sandbox in September 2019 and moved to Incubating in April 2022, and that it has "2,088 contributors across 520 contributing organizations" since launch, according to [CNCF](https://www.cncf.io/announcements/2026/11/10/cloud-native-computing-foundation-announces-kubevirt-graduation/). It lists ByteDance, Cloudflare, CoreWeave and NVIDIA among production adopters.

The NATS release says the project was accepted at the Incubating level in March 2018 and now has "6,377 contributors from 1,674 organizations", more than 400 million downloads and over 2,000 companies using it, according to [CNCF](https://www.cncf.io/announcements/2026/11/10/cncf-announces-nats-graduation-solidifying-a-production-ready-foundation-for-messaging-across-cloud-and-edge/).

[SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/12/kubevirt-graduates-from-cncf-kubernetes-unites-vms-ai-kubeconna/) published a theCUBE interview with Fabian Deutsch, whom it describes as a KubeVirt maintainer at Red Hat, and a [second segment](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/13/data-center-telemetry-runs-nats-graduates-from-cncf-kubeconna/) with two NATS maintainers it places at Synadia. Each page discloses that the Linux Foundation sponsored the segment and says sponsors have no editorial control. Neither page gives the shares discussed below.

## The TOC record, in order

Times are the UTC timestamps GitHub's API returns for the [KubeVirt](https://github.com/cncf/toc/issues/1822) and [NATS](https://github.com/cncf/toc/issues/2042) issues.

- 2025-08-14: [KubeVirt's application issue](https://github.com/cncf/toc/issues/1822) is opened. On 2025-09-10 a reply in the issue says the queue is first-in, first-out, with 7 projects ahead of KubeVirt.
- 2026-02-17: [NATS's application issue](https://github.com/cncf/toc/issues/2042) is opened.
- 2026-10-30: the NATS vote is called in [issue 2042](https://github.com/cncf/toc/issues/2042). It closes on 2026-11-02 at 07:24 with nine in favor, none against, no abstentions and two not voting (81.82% against a 66% threshold).
- 2026-11-02: the KubeVirt vote is called in [issue 1822](https://github.com/cncf/toc/issues/1822). It closes on 2026-11-04 at 09:30 with nine in favor, none against, one abstention and one not voting (81.82%).
- 2026-11-10: CNCF issues both announcements, [KubeVirt](https://www.cncf.io/announcements/2026/11/10/cloud-native-computing-foundation-announces-kubevirt-graduation/) and [NATS](https://www.cncf.io/announcements/2026/11/10/cncf-announces-nats-graduation-solidifying-a-production-ready-foundation-for-messaging-across-cloud-and-edge/).

By the Herald's own arithmetic, the applications took 447 days (KubeVirt) and 258 days (NATS) from opening to the closing of the vote, and the announcements followed the votes by 6 and 8 days.

## What the due diligence documents say

The [KubeVirt document](https://github.com/cncf/toc/blob/66896801e461d70b6492c1830a17d57f5b7b52ed/projects/kubevirt/kubevirt-graduation-due-diligence.md) calls its technical and governance reviews satisfactory and recommends graduation. It also says six of the eight maintainers work for Red Hat, enough to meet both the simple-majority voting rule and the two-thirds threshold for governance changes, and calls that concentration "worth addressing". For October 1, 2025 through October 1, 2026 it reports, from LFX Insights, 697 active contributors, 121 reviewers and 123 active organizations, with Red Hat accounting for 71,874 contribution activities, about 91% on the LFX organization leaderboard. It counts activities such as commits, pull requests and issue participation. A maintainer-diversity plan is a non-blocking one-year goal, and the document recommends a follow-up with the TOC at one year. The [tracking issue](https://github.com/kubevirt/community/issues/468) was still open when the Herald read it on 2026-11-15, and the project's [MAINTAINERS.md](https://github.com/kubevirt/community/blob/b0d2ed631fceaa2485d13cf5b30551f33fdb9e67/MAINTAINERS.md) at commit b0d2ed6 lists eight maintainers, six employed by Red Hat, one by Google and one by Nvidia (a Herald count).

The [NATS document](https://github.com/cncf/toc/blob/4c83a0f63116b98fefc0c63862bbd1b51558c53c/projects/nats/nats-graduation-dd.md) reports that LFX Insights shows two organizations accounting for 67% of contributions and six contributors for 55%, with Synadia at 50.39% on the organization leaderboard, and presents them as data to weigh against the project's organization-balanced voting model, under which maintainers from one organization share a single vote and independent maintainers vote individually. It says the governance review's initial status was "Mostly Satisfactory" and that the TOC found the criteria satisfied after project-side remediation, including website and documentation changes to reduce Synadia-specific presentation. None of its five listed non-blocking recommendations concerns contributor diversity. NATS's [MAINTAINERS.md](https://github.com/nats-io/nats-general/blob/9d7ee235089cb5fd8077e18b6e12b8945fe41101/MAINTAINERS.md) at commit 9d7ee23 lists 24 maintainers, nine of them affiliated with Synadia and five listed as independent (a Herald count).

## Figures that differ in scope

CNCF's announcements and the TOC documents use different windows and scopes, and none of the cited sources reconciles them. The KubeVirt release counts 2,088 contributors since launch; the document counts 697 active contributors over one year. The NATS release cites 6,377 contributors; the due diligence document says its re-checks recorded 214 GitHub contributors for the `nats-server` repository, and LFX Insights reported 24 maintainers and 57 reviewers over the past 365 days.

## Context: the 2025 NATS dispute

[The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2025/04/28/cncf-tells-nats-contributor-synadia-its-free-to-fork-off/1042739) reported on April 28, 2025 that CNCF had filed petitions with the US Patent and Trademark Office to prevent Synadia from using the NATS logo and domain name after Synadia decided to move future server releases from Apache 2.0 to the Business Source License, and Synadia's lawyers wrote to the Linux Foundation asking to end the relationship and receive control of the NATS.io domain and GitHub repository. Derek Collison, Synadia's founder and CEO, replied in a blog post quoted by The Register that NATS is "primarily funded, supported, and maintained by Synadia". On May 1, 2025, [CNCF and Synadia announced](https://www.cncf.io/announcements/2025/05/01/cncf-and-synadia-align-on-securing-the-future-of-the-nats-io-project/) that NATS would stay in CNCF, that Synadia would assign two trademark registrations to the Linux Foundation, and that the project's assets would remain with CNCF under the Apache-2.0 license. The NATS due diligence document does not mention the dispute.

## What is not known

The TOC documents give the shares as LFX Insights figures for specific windows; the Herald did not recompute them. Apart from the TOC documents, none of the sources cited here gives a figure for Red Hat's or Synadia's share, and none says how the TOC weighed them beyond the documents' own text. The KubeVirt document's one-year follow-up has no date in the sources.
