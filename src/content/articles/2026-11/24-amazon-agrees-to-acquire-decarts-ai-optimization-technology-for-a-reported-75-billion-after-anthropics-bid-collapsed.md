## Overview

Amazon has agreed to acquire the core technology and founding team of Decart, an Israeli AI startup, in a deal reported at $7.5 billion, according to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/23/amazon-snaps-up-decarts-ai-optimization-tech-for-7-5b-as-it-prepares-to-sell-trainium-chips/) and [SiliconAngle](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/23/amazon-acquires-key-assets-of-ai-startup-decart-for-reported-7-5b/), both citing Bloomberg sources. Amazon has not disclosed the financial terms of the transaction, according to [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/5vfuvprn2). The deal follows an earlier, unsuccessful attempt by Anthropic to buy the startup for a reported $6 billion.

## What Amazon Is Acquiring

Amazon's announcement said it had agreed to acquire the proprietary technology behind Decart's Optimization Stack, known as DOS, and bring the company's founders and AI researchers and engineers into Amazon, according to [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/5vfuvprn2). Decart's founders, Dean Leitersdorf, Moshe Shalev and Orian Leitersdorf, will join Amazon along with the company's research team, [SiliconAngle](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/23/amazon-acquires-key-assets-of-ai-startup-decart-for-reported-7-5b/) reported. Decart's internally developed AI models, including Lucy 2.5 and Oasis 3, were not part of the acquisition, according to [SiliconAngle](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/23/amazon-acquires-key-assets-of-ai-startup-decart-for-reported-7-5b/).

DOS optimizes AI models through kernel tuning and proprietary compilers, using techniques such as kernel fusion to combine multiple code snippets and reduce hardware usage, [SiliconAngle](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/23/amazon-acquires-key-assets-of-ai-startup-decart-for-reported-7-5b/) reported. According to Decart, the software can reduce the cost of running some neural networks by a hundredfold, [SiliconAngle](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/23/amazon-acquires-key-assets-of-ai-startup-decart-for-reported-7-5b/) reported.

Decart had been working with Amazon since 2025, helping optimize the performance of Amazon's Trainium chips, according to [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/5vfuvprn2). That collaboration extended to Trainium3, which delivers 2.52 petaflops for FP8 processing, [SiliconAngle](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/23/amazon-acquires-key-assets-of-ai-startup-decart-for-reported-7-5b/) reported. Amazon's senior vice president overseeing foundational AI models, custom silicon and quantum computing, Peter DeSantis, said: "Every leap forward in AI starts with getting more out of every chip," according to [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/5vfuvprn2). DeSantis separately said: "More efficient compute means better training, better reinforcement learning, faster inference, and better models for customers at a lower cost," according to [SiliconAngle](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/23/amazon-acquires-key-assets-of-ai-startup-decart-for-reported-7-5b/).

## A Short, Eventful History

Decart was founded in September 2023 by Dean Leitersdorf and Moshe Shalev, who met while serving in the Israeli military's Unit 8200, with Orian Leitersdorf later joining the founding team, according to [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/5vfuvprn2). The company's original focus was GPU optimization software that it licensed to cloud providers and other customers, [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/5vfuvprn2) reported.

The startup raised a $21 million seed round in October 2024 led by Sequoia Capital, followed by a $32 million round led by Benchmark the following month at a valuation of more than $500 million, according to [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/5vfuvprn2). Decart gained wider attention that same period with Oasis, an AI-generated interactive virtual world that the company said attracted five million users in its first week, [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/5vfuvprn2) reported.

In August 2025, Decart raised $100 million at a $3.1 billion valuation, bringing its total funding at the time to $153 million, with new investor Aleph joining existing backers Sequoia, Benchmark and Zeev Ventures, according to [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/5vfuvprn2). By May 2026, the company had raised another $300 million at an estimated $4 billion valuation in a round led by Radical Ventures that included Nvidia, eBay Ventures, Adobe Ventures, Toyota Ventures and Atreides Management, pushing total funding past $450 million, [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/5vfuvprn2) reported. The following month, Decart introduced Oasis 3, an interactive world model for generating simulated environments including driving scenarios for testing autonomous vehicles and other physical AI systems, according to [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/5vfuvprn2).

The reported $7.5 billion price is nearly double Decart's May valuation, [SiliconAngle](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/23/amazon-acquires-key-assets-of-ai-startup-decart-for-reported-7-5b/) reported.

## A Bidding History That Included Nvidia and Anthropic

Amazon was not the first major company to pursue Decart. Nvidia had previously shown interest in acquiring the startup, but those discussions did not lead to an agreement, according to [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/5vfuvprn2). Anthropic later entered advanced negotiations for a deal reportedly worth around $6 billion; the two sides exchanged draft agreements and Anthropic was reported to have largely completed due diligence, but the talks fell apart in early September, [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/5vfuvprn2) reported. [SiliconAngle](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/23/amazon-acquires-key-assets-of-ai-startup-decart-for-reported-7-5b/) similarly reported that Anthropic attempted to acquire Decart for $6 billion in August but withdrew in September before Amazon completed its own deal. It is not clear what ultimately dissuaded Anthropic from pursuing the acquisition, [SiliconAngle](https://siliconangle.com/2026/10/11/report-amazon-in-talks-with-ai-startup-decart-over-possible-7b-acquisition/) reported in an earlier account of the talks.

Decart then tried to revive discussions with Nvidia, which declined to reopen negotiations, before Amazon emerged as the buyer, according to [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/5vfuvprn2). Amazon had already been in talks with Decart over a possible acquisition worth up to $7 billion as early as October, [SiliconAngle](https://siliconangle.com/2026/10/11/report-amazon-in-talks-with-ai-startup-decart-over-possible-7b-acquisition/) reported at the time, noting that Decart had obtained early access to Trainium3 chips the previous year after a meeting with senior AWS engineers.

Under the terms of the final deal, Decart's founders have agreed to remain at Amazon for five years, with a significant portion of the reported consideration deferred until later in that period, according to [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/5vfuvprn2).

## Why It Matters for Amazon's Chip Strategy

Amazon has invested heavily in Trainium, its custom AI processor line designed to train and run models at lower cost than relying solely on Nvidia hardware, [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/5vfuvprn2) reported, and is preparing to sell the chips more broadly to customers as an alternative to Nvidia, [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/23/amazon-snaps-up-decarts-ai-optimization-tech-for-7-5b-as-it-prepares-to-sell-trainium-chips/) reported. Decart's software is designed to work across different hardware platforms, including Nvidia GPUs, Google's TPUs and Amazon's own processors, which cloud providers see as useful for offering customers alternatives to Nvidia, according to [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/5vfuvprn2).
