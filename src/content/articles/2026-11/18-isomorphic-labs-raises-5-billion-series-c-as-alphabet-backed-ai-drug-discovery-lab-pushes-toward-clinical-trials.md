## Overview

Isomorphic Labs, the AI drug-discovery company spun out of Google's DeepMind in 2021, has raised $5 billion in a Series C funding round as it works to move its AI-designed drug candidates into clinical trials, according to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/isomorphic-labs-raises-5b-as-it-looks-to-take-drug-candidates-to-clinical-trials/). The round was co-led by Greenoaks and Sequoia Capital, with new investor Andreessen Horowitz joining alongside continued participation from Alphabet and the company's existing backers, according to [Isomorphic Labs' press release](https://www.prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/isomorphic-labs-announces-5-billion-series-c-funding-302942093.html).

## What We Know

The $5 billion round came six months after Isomorphic closed a $2.1 billion Series B in May, according to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/isomorphic-labs-raises-5b-as-it-looks-to-take-drug-candidates-to-clinical-trials/) and [R&D World](https://www.rdworldonline.com/isomorphic-labs-raises-5-billion-in-biotechs-biggest-venture-round/). Beyond Greenoaks, Sequoia Capital and Andreessen Horowitz, the round included all of the company's existing investors: Alphabet's venture arms GV and CapitalG, Thrive Capital, MGX, Temasek and the UK Sovereign AI Fund, according to [Isomorphic Labs' press release](https://www.prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/isomorphic-labs-announces-5-billion-series-c-funding-302942093.html).

Isomorphic builds what it calls a drug design engine, named IsoDDE, that uses AI simulation models to explore chemistry and design new medicines, according to the [press release](https://www.prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/isomorphic-labs-announces-5-billion-series-c-funding-302942093.html). Its work builds on AlphaFold, the protein-structure-prediction system developed at DeepMind, and the company says its program has yielded "positive preclinical results" in oncology and immunology, according to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/isomorphic-labs-raises-5b-as-it-looks-to-take-drug-candidates-to-clinical-trials/). Isomorphic has struck drug-discovery deals with Eli Lilly, Novartis and Johnson & Johnson, the outlet reported. The company is headquartered in London, with additional offices in Cambridge, Massachusetts, and Lausanne, Switzerland, according to the [press release](https://www.prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/isomorphic-labs-announces-5-billion-series-c-funding-302942093.html).

The new funding is Isomorphic's first fundraise since founder and CEO Demis Hassabis stepped down as head of DeepMind to focus on drug discovery, a move that also saw him take on a chairman role at DeepMind, according to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/isomorphic-labs-raises-5b-as-it-looks-to-take-drug-candidates-to-clinical-trials/). Hassabis and then-DeepMind director John Jumper won the Nobel Prize in chemistry for their work on AlphaFold. Jumper has since left DeepMind for Anthropic, as [The Machine Herald previously reported](/article/2026-06/23-nobel-laureate-john-jumper-leaves-google-deepmind-for-anthropic-the-second-ai-star-to-exit-google-in-a-week).

Isomorphic did not disclose a new valuation alongside the Series C, according to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/isomorphic-labs-raises-5b-as-it-looks-to-take-drug-candidates-to-clinical-trials/). Both TechCrunch and [R&D World](https://www.rdworldonline.com/isomorphic-labs-raises-5-billion-in-biotechs-biggest-venture-round/) reported that Bloomberg had said earlier in November that the company was in talks to raise funding at a valuation of roughly $50 billion — a figure neither outlet confirmed independently of the Bloomberg report. R&D World also cited UK Companies House records reflecting a June share allotment as showing Alphabet holds 75% or more of Isomorphic's shares and voting rights, and cited a BioSpace tally putting Isomorphic's cumulative funding since March 2025 at least $7.7 billion. The same R&D World report cited BioSpace's description of the $5 billion round as the largest venture round in biotech history, surpassing the $3 billion Altos Labs raised at its 2022 launch.

R&D World also reported that Hassabis said at Davos in January that Isomorphic expected its first clinical trials by the end of 2026, according to Reuters reporting cited in the same article — a target that replaced his earlier goal of putting AI-designed drugs into trials by the end of 2025.

## What We Don't Know

Isomorphic has not disclosed an official post-money valuation for the Series C round; the $50 billion figure circulating in coverage traces to a Bloomberg report on funding talks rather than a confirmed number. It is also not yet clear whether Isomorphic's first clinical trial will in fact begin by the end of 2026, as Hassabis projected in January, or which of its drug candidates — across oncology, immunology or other areas — will be first to reach that stage.

## Analysis

The round underscores how much capital AI drug-discovery efforts tied to Alphabet are attracting even without a single approved drug yet in human trials. Isomorphic's model of using AI to design drug candidates and then partnering with established pharmaceutical companies — rather than running its own full clinical and commercial operation from scratch — has so far drawn repeat investment from Alphabet alongside venture firms including Sequoia Capital and, newly, Andreessen Horowitz.
