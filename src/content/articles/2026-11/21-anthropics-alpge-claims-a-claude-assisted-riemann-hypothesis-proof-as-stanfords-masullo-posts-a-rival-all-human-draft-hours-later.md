## Background

The Riemann hypothesis, first posed in 1859, is one of mathematics' most famous unsolved problems, and one of the few on which the Clay Mathematics Institute has placed a $1 million prize, according to [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/tech/did-claude-ai-crack-mathematics-1-million-riemann-hypothesis-anthropic-mathematician-makes-big-claim). A verified solution must be peer-reviewed and withstand two years of scrutiny before the Clay Institute will pay out, according to [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-anthropic-claude-math-greatest-riddle.html).

Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, is not new to AI-assisted mathematics. In July 2026, Alpöge used an earlier Claude model to disprove the 87-year-old Jacobian conjecture with a counterexample short enough to fit in a single social-media post, as [previously reported](/article/2026-07/22-anthropic-mathematician-uses-claude-fable-5-to-disprove-the-87-year-old-jacobian-conjecture-in-a-216-character-post). That result drew a mixed reaction from mathematicians at the time, split between those who saw real insight in the search strategy and those who saw a search problem well-suited to machines.

The new claim lands one day after Anthropic's initial public offering, which priced at $83 a share on November 18, 2026 and valued the company at roughly $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis, as [previously reported](/article/2026-11/19-anthropic-prices-ipo-at-83-a-share-raising-813-billion-in-wall-streets-largest-ever-offering). [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03812-7) reports that Anthropic's valuation declined on November 19, the day after the offering priced.

## Current situation

Alpöge announced on his personal website that he had, in his words, "working with Claude, discovered a proof of the Riemann hypothesis," according to [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-anthropic-claude-math-greatest-riddle.html). He posted a paper and accompanying computer code rather than a traditional journal submission, according to [THE DECODER](https://the-decoder.com/hours-after-anthropic-claims-a-claude-proof-of-the-riemann-hypothesis-stanford-releases-an-all-human-competing-draft/), and the underlying argument is written in the Lean formal-proof language rather than natural-language mathematical prose. Printed out, Claude's Lean argument would run over 250,000 pages, [THE DECODER reported](https://the-decoder.com/hours-after-anthropic-claims-a-claude-proof-of-the-riemann-hypothesis-stanford-releases-an-all-human-competing-draft/). Alpöge has said the method also resolves the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, a broader statement than the original problem, according to both [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-anthropic-claude-math-greatest-riddle.html) and [THE DECODER](https://the-decoder.com/hours-after-anthropic-claims-a-claude-proof-of-the-riemann-hypothesis-stanford-releases-an-all-human-competing-draft/), which independently confirm the claim in their own reporting.

Alpöge has acknowledged that Claude moved faster than he could follow in parts of the argument, telling Phys.org he had not yet mastered all of the mathematics involved, and [THE DECODER reported](https://the-decoder.com/hours-after-anthropic-claims-a-claude-proof-of-the-riemann-hypothesis-stanford-releases-an-all-human-competing-draft/) that he is presenting a result whose surrounding mathematics he has not fully mastered. Neither the Lean code nor any accompanying written argument has undergone peer review, according to [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-anthropic-claude-math-greatest-riddle.html).

Reaction from mathematicians has split along different lines than Alpöge's earlier Jacobian conjecture result. Brian Conrey, described by [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-anthropic-claude-math-greatest-riddle.html) as a leading Riemann hypothesis specialist, called it "really the biggest thing I think that's ever happened in mathematics," while cautioning that he had seen only Alpöge's posted remarks rather than the full manuscript. Fields Medal winner James Maynard struck a more conditional tone, telling [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03812-7): "If this fully checks out, it is clearly a historic milestone in mathematics." Number theorist Andrew Granville was openly skeptical, telling Nature: "What he released is garbled," referring to a four-page document Alpöge posted separately from the Lean code, according to [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03812-7). Granville also raised the timing of the announcement relative to Anthropic's stock debut, which Alpöge rejected, saying: "There is an obvious cynical view, to do with the timing, but it is false," according to [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03812-7).

Hours after Alpöge's announcement, Stanford released a competing, all-human draft proof by postdoctoral researcher Alessandro Masullo, according to [THE DECODER](https://the-decoder.com/hours-after-anthropic-claims-a-claude-proof-of-the-riemann-hypothesis-stanford-releases-an-all-human-competing-draft/), a release [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-anthropic-claude-math-greatest-riddle.html) separately attributed to Stanford mathematician Ravi Vakil, who said a postdoctoral researcher in his department had independently submitted a competing proof. Stanford number theorist Brian Conrad — a different person from Phys.org's Brian Conrey — had reviewed Masullo's draft quietly for months before its release, according to [THE DECODER](https://the-decoder.com/hours-after-anthropic-claims-a-claude-proof-of-the-riemann-hypothesis-stanford-releases-an-all-human-competing-draft/). Masullo had originally planned to spend a year refining the draft at Stanford before making it public, but Alpöge's announcement prompted an accelerated release, according to [THE DECODER](https://the-decoder.com/hours-after-anthropic-claims-a-claude-proof-of-the-riemann-hypothesis-stanford-releases-an-all-human-competing-draft/). Alpöge and Claude's approach extended an idea from mathematician Stepanov, who had solved a related question in a different mathematical setting, [THE DECODER reported](https://the-decoder.com/hours-after-anthropic-claims-a-claude-proof-of-the-riemann-hypothesis-stanford-releases-an-all-human-competing-draft/).

## Implications

The two proofs' verification paths cut against a simple AI-versus-human framing. Masullo's draft, though billed as all-human, has itself drawn scrutiny with AI assistance: several mathematicians contacted him to report problems in the proof that they had identified with help from different AI models, according to [THE DECODER](https://the-decoder.com/hours-after-anthropic-claims-a-claude-proof-of-the-riemann-hypothesis-stanford-releases-an-all-human-competing-draft/). That leaves both the machine-assisted and the human-authored claim passing through some form of AI-aided review before either reaches the community-wide scrutiny the Clay Institute requires.

The stakes of a confirmed proof extend well beyond the $1 million prize. Hundreds of published papers assume the Riemann hypothesis is true without proving it, and a confirmed proof would turn all of those into established theorems overnight, mathematician Kontorovich told [THE DECODER](https://the-decoder.com/hours-after-anthropic-claims-a-claude-proof-of-the-riemann-hypothesis-stanford-releases-an-all-human-competing-draft/). [THE DECODER also reported](https://the-decoder.com/hours-after-anthropic-claims-a-claude-proof-of-the-riemann-hypothesis-stanford-releases-an-all-human-competing-draft/) that all outstanding issues in either proof would need to be resolved to the satisfaction of the mathematical community before it could be accepted as true — a process neither Phys.org, THE DECODER, Nature, nor Free Press Journal reported as complete or underway on a fixed timeline for either claim.

Alpöge's Lean-compiled proof poses its own distinct verification challenge: a 250,000-page argument that compiles successfully in a formal proof assistant is not the same thing as an argument a human mathematician has read and understood end to end, and Granville's complaint that Alpöge's own four-page summary is "garbled" suggests the gap between what Claude produced and what Alpöge can currently explain in plain mathematical language remains wide, according to [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03812-7). None of the sources reviewed here report that any mathematician outside Alpöge and Anthropic has yet read the full Lean argument.

For Anthropic, the claim arrives at a moment of intense public attention, a day after its stock market debut, which gives Granville's timing question — and Alpöge's denial of it — added weight regardless of how the mathematics eventually resolves.
